IRF1 — directional inconsistency in mechanistic literature (CONTESTED)

I'm marking **IRF1's mechanistic-directionality narrative CONTESTED** (not IRF1's candidacy per se, which remains a live WEAKENED candidate): Hey et al. 2021 (enhanced Irf1 → excessive inflammation) and Veerati et al. 2020 (delayed/blunted IRF1-dependent antiviral induction in COPD donor cells) point toward opposite functional directions of IRF1 dysregulation in COPD-relevant tissue, and the researcher's synthesis does not flag this tension. This is not a new citation (both already VERIFIED) so nothing needs to go back to the citation-verifier — it's a synthesis-level contradiction, preserved here for the audit trail.

Specifically: Hey J, et al. Nat Commun 2021 (PMID 34764283) found enhanced Irf1 function/expression in airway macrophages of Scnn1b-transgenic mice impairs efferocytosis and produces excessive inflammatory responses (a gain-of-function-flavored, pro-inflammatory phenotype). Veerati PC, et al. Front Immunol 2020 (PMID 32499788) found rhinovirus-induced antiviral gene induction (including IRF1) is delayed, not deficient, in COPD/asthma donor airway epithelial cells relative to healthy donors (a blunted-response phenotype). These point in opposite functional directions and were not reconciled by any source found in this review.

Unresolved alternative hypothesis flagged by red-team-skeptic (locus-wide, not gene-specific)

This is the most significant gap in the researcher's writeup. Chr5q31.1 (132.15-132.75 Mb, containing IL3/IL4/IL5/IL13/CSF2) is one of the most extensively replicated asthma/atopy/ eosinophil-count/total-IgE loci in human genetics (the classical "5q31 cytokine gene cluster" asthma locus). The researcher's own evidence gestures at this without connecting it: - IRF1's asthma-GWAS L2G "edges out" SLC22A5 per the structured context. - KIF3A is explicitly noted to have asthma/atopic-dermatitis genetics at a "different sub-locus" nearby. - IL5's high clinical score is driven by eosinophilic-COPD biologic trials — eosinophilic COPD substantially overlaps clinically and immunologically with asthma/ACOS. - Several IRF1 papers cited are explicitly asthma-oriented (Matsuzaki et al., Veerati et al.'s COPD/asthma donor comparison, Koetzler et al.'s asthma/COPD framing). Given that COPD GWAS case ascertainment (especially registry/ICD-code-based, e.g., FinnGen) is prone to including asthma-COPD overlap syndrome cases without spirometry-confirmed airflow obstruction, there is a live alternative hypothesis that part or all of this locus's "COPD" signal is driven by asthma/atopy biology and comorbid ACOS misclassification rather than a COPD/emphysema-specific mechanism — for **any** of the candidate genes, not just KIF3A. The researcher did not raise or rule this out.

This was not independently resolved by any further search in this pipeline run (the red-team-skeptic disclosed its own search budget was exhausted before it could run a confirmatory contradiction-search pass — see below) and should be treated by the study team as an open question for this locus.
