Audit Report - Locus 4:66100001-67250000 (nearest gene CENPC)

This file preserves everything excluded from the main evidence table (`evidence_tables/4_66100001-67250000.md`), plus the full reasoning trail, so the evidence table itself stays clean for distribution while this remains available for review.

Excluded citations

**PMID 9914371 — Figueroa J et al., "Microinjection of antibodies to centromere protein CENP-A arrests cells in interphase but does not prevent mitosis." Chromosoma. 1998.**

- Originally proposed by the researcher as evidence that CENP-C is a recognized autoantigen in CREST syndrome/scleroderma (an autoimmune connective-tissue disease), intended to probe whether CENPC has any immune-relevant biology. - Citation-verifier verdict: **CLAIM_MISMATCH**. The paper's title, abstract, and experimental focus concern CENP-A, not CENP-C. The abstract does mention "CENP-E and CENP-C autoantigens" in passing, but the paper's actual subject and findings are about CENP-A's role in the cell cycle, not a CENP-C-specific finding. - The researcher itself had already judged this line of evidence (CENP-C as CREST autoantigen) as not relevant to COPD immunopathology before the mismatch was confirmed, so its exclusion does not remove any evidence that was otherwise going to weigh in favor of CENPC's immune relevance — it simply confirms there is no valid immunology citation for CENPC in this dossier at all.

No other citations were excluded. All other 8 citations produced by the researcher (PMID 30643251, 30804561, 37067150, 28125646, 35860812, 36911742, 31583695, 21753007) received a final **VERIFIED** verdict, including after a targeted re-verification pass described below.

Verification process note: duplicate-quote flag and resolution

The red-team-skeptic noticed that the citation-verifier's first-pass justification for two separate EPHA5 citations — PMID 36911742 (Li M et al., Front Immunol 2023, TCGA pan-cancer TMB analysis) and PMID 31583695 (Qian J et al., Int J Cancer 2019, NSCLC/atezolizumab cohort) — quoted the *identical* sentence ("TP53, KEAP1, APC, RB1, CREBBP, EPHA5 and STAG2 mutations were associated with OS"), raising the possibility that one or both papers had not been independently checked.

A second, independent citation-verifier pass retrieved each paper's own text separately and found: - PMID 36911742's actual EPHA5-relevant sentence is "The 12 genes included ARID1A, ARID1B, BRIP1, NOTCH2, NOTCH4, EPHA5, ROS1, FAT1, SPEN, NSD1, PTPRT, and ZFHX3" (a distinct 12-gene panel from a pan-cancer immune-checkpoint-blockade cohort). - PMID 31583695's actual sentence is indeed "TP53, KEAP1, APC, RB1, CREBBP, EPHA5 and STAG2 mutations were associated with OS" (from the NSCLC/atezolizumab ctDNA cohort).

Conclusion: the duplicate quote was a **write-up artifact in the first verification pass** (citation 7's justification had mistakenly reused citation 8's quoted sentence), not an error in either paper's identity, existence, or the claim attributed to it. Both citations are confirmed genuine and independently supported on re-check and retain **VERIFIED** status. This is preserved here rather than silently corrected, per the instruction to keep an honest, traceable audit trail — the underlying papers and claims were fine, but the verification process needed a second pass to actually confirm that.

Contested / weakened gene-level verdicts (red-team-skeptic, verbatim reasoning)

**EPHA5 — marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic.**

Reasoning (verbatim from critique): "No GWAS/L2G database support at all (a genuine null across 16 independent OT credible sets, functioning as contradicting evidence against physical/regulatory linkage to this locus), no COPD/spirometric literature, physically farther from the signal than CENPC, and mechanistic biology confined to neurodevelopment. The only immune-adjacent literature is tangential, somatic, cancer-specific, and now additionally under a cloud due to the duplicate-quote verification concern [since resolved — see above]. CONTESTED — not because a paper argues against EPHA5 specifically, but because the combination of (a) an explicit, systematic null result across all of Open Targets' GWAS/QTL evidence for this gene at this locus, and (b) the compromised/duplicate- quote state of its strongest immune-related citations, means the case for prioritizing EPHA5 here does not hold up; CENPC (itself weak) is still better supported than EPHA5 on every axis (proximity, L2G, colocalization)."

Note: point (b) of this reasoning (the duplicate-quote concern) has since been resolved by independent re-verification (see above) — both EPHA5 immunology citations are genuine and independently supported. Point (a) (the systematic absence of any GWAS/L2G/colocalization evidence for EPHA5 at this locus) stands unaffected and remains the primary basis for the CONTESTED verdict.

**CENPC — marked WEAKENED, explicitly flagged as "borderline CONTESTED" by red-team-skeptic.**

Reasoning (verbatim from critique): "The proximity/FLAMES nomination is real, but the inference that CENPC is a lung-intrinsic COPD gene overreaches substantially: it sits outside the stated locus window in a gene desert, its OT L2G confidence is sub-threshold, there is zero COPD/lung-function literature, and — most importantly — the locus's own colocalization profile points toward smoking-behavior mediation rather than lung biology, with CENPC's genome-wide top hit being 'smoking initiation' itself. This is close to CONTESTED (the colocalization data functions as quantitative contradicting evidence against a lung-intrinsic CENPC mechanism, even though no paper explicitly argues against CENPC or names a rival gene) but I'll call it WEAKENED, with the explicit caveat that it is borderline CONTESTED: treat CENPC as a low-confidence, likely smoking-mediated nearest-gene call rather than a mechanistically supported COPD gene."

Also flagged by the skeptic: calling CENPC the "nearest gene" for this locus is "technically true only in the loosest sense" since its transcript start (67,468,762) falls outside the locus's own stated upper boundary (67,250,000) — a labeling overreach worth noting independent of the underlying biology.

Search-coverage gap disclosed by red-team-skeptic (verbatim)

"Before critiquing, a transparency note: my three intended contradiction-search queries (CENPC+COPD, EPHA5+COPD, rs13109017+smoking) all failed — the WebSearch tool reported the session's search budget was already exhausted (200/200) before I could run them. I then tried WebFetch against PubMed and Europe PMC for the two flagged EPHA5 citations (PMID 36911742, 31583695) to check the orchestrator's duplicate-quote concern directly; both were blocked by cookie/navigation walls and returned no content. So neither CENPC nor EPHA5 got any independent search pass from me this round — my critique below rests entirely on the researcher's findings, the citation-verifier's verdicts, and the orchestrator's Open Targets structured data, not on new literature I found myself. This is a real gap; if search capacity frees up, a genuine contradiction-search pass on both genes (and a live re-check of the two EPHA5 PMIDs) should still be done before this locus is finalized."

The follow-up re-verification pass (see above) did independently confirm the two EPHA5 citations directly, partially closing this gap, but a fresh independent contradiction-search pass on CENPC and EPHA5 (beyond what the original researcher and this audit already cover) was not performed for this locus and remains an open item for any future review of this locus.

Locus-specific overlap in landmark GWAS papers — unresolved gap

Two verified citations (PMID 30804561, the Sakornsakolpat 2019 COPD GWAS reporting 82 loci; and PMID 30643251, the Liu 2019 GSCAN smoking/alcohol GWAS reporting 406 loci) could not be confirmed, from their abstracts alone, to actually include chr4:66.1-67.25 Mb among their reported genome-wide-significant loci. This is flagged as an open gap rather than asserted either way in the main evidence table, consistent with both the researcher's and citation-verifier's explicit caveats.
