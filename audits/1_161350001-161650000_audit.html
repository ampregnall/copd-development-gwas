Genes marked CONTESTED or WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (verbatim reasoning preserved)

HSPA6 — CONTESTED

"The researcher's own summary ('generic, highly inducible stress-response gene... no COPD-specific literature') is honest, not overclaimed. But I'd go further than 'weak': the one piece of *functional* annotation actually available for the lead variant rs2099684 (Yazar et al. 2026, PMID 42102734) is an eQTL for the *neighboring* gene FCGR3A, not HSPA6 — that's not merely absent evidence for HSPA6, it's a specific finding that actively points the regulatory mechanism at this exact variant away from HSPA6. Combine that with Open Targets' cross-trait L2G (blood counts, albumin, eGFR, Takayasu, hematocrit, macrophage/GTEx QTLs) never once naming HSPA6 as top gene despite tagging the same locus repeatedly, and the case that HSPA6's prioritization is nearest-gene/PoPS/ABC artifact rather than true biology becomes fairly strong. The irritant-inducibility data (cigarette smoke condensate in BEAS-2B, sulfur mustard in A549) is real but is generic heat-shock-family stress response (HSPA6 is one of many genes induced under xenobiotic/oxidative stress in almost any epithelial cell line) — it doesn't distinguish HSPA6 as a lung-specific or COPD-specific mechanism versus a nonspecific stress-response bystander. No lung-function GWAS support compounds this (prior #2).

Verdict: CONTESTED. Not merely under-supported — there is a specific competing finding (the lead variant's own published eQTL target is the neighboring gene, not HSPA6) that argues against HSPA6 being the effector gene at this locus."

FCGR3A — WEAKENED

"The researcher's hedged framing ('rests on plausible immune mechanism... plus an eQTL/statistical-genetics link... rather than any direct COPD phenotype evidence') is appropriately conservative and matches the underlying evidence — no overclaiming in the researcher's own language. But several things deserve more skepticism than even that hedge implies:

- Tissue mismatch. The rs2099684→FCGR3A eQTL (Yazar et al. 2026) is NK-cell-specific and, per the study's design (single-cell eQTL, sex-interacting), is derived from blood/PBMC data, not lung tissue. NK cells and CD16+ monocytes do traffic through lung, but a blood eQTL is not lung-tissue evidence of effect direction or magnitude in situ; this should be weighted as suggestive-of-mechanism, not disease-tissue-confirmed. - Phenotype analogy problem. Takayasu arteritis (large-vessel autoimmune vasculitis) and malaria-antibody response are being used as supportive precedent for 'this variant matters for immune-mediated disease,' but neither is COPD or even a lung phenotype. Pleiotropy of an FCGR-cluster regulatory variant across autoimmune and infectious-disease traits establishes that the variant is immunologically active in general — it does not establish that its COPD association (if real) operates through the same mechanism, cell type, or even the same gene. This is an analogy, not corroborating disease evidence, and should be labeled as such. - Within that analogy, the evidence is already shakier than presented: the Takayasu signal is explicitly reported as joint 'FCGR2A/FCGR3A,' not FCGR3A alone, and in the malaria literature the researcher notes rs2099684 itself was *not* the variant reaching significance in one of the two cited studies (Cretin et al., a different variant, rs1771575, was). Citing Cretin et al. as evidence 'for' rs2099684 is a soft overclaim worth flagging even though the citation is verified as existing and accurately quoted — the researcher's inclusion of it under 'FCGR3A evidence' risks being read as more supportive than it is. - Paralog confounding is unresolved and is the single biggest problem. FCGR3A, FCGR3B, and FCGR2A are tandemly duplicated, highly homologous, and FCGR3B carries a common CNV — no cited paper cleanly isolates FCGR3A's effect from FCGR3B/FCGR2A at this variant. MAGMA (the only tool prioritizing FCGR3A internally) is a gene-based test that sums signal in a window and is known to perform poorly at distinguishing tightly linked paralogous genes — its prioritization of FCGR3A here could just as easily be capturing FCGR3B or FCGR2A signal. - One mitigating point in FCGR3A's favor: no overlap was found with smoking-behavior GWAS signals, which argues against a smoking-neurobiology confound (prior #1) — if the signal is real, it's more likely immune/lung-cell-intrinsic than smoking-mediated. But the absence of any lung-function (FEV1/FEV1/FVC) association (prior #2) is a real gap that the researcher didn't downplay but that should be weighted explicitly against causality.

Verdict: WEAKENED. FCGR3A has a genuine molecular anchor (a specific, cell-type-resolved eQTL at the lead variant) that HSPA6 lacks, which is a real point in its favor relative to HSPA6. But 'immune plausibility + eQTL in the wrong tissue + disease analogies from unrelated autoimmune/infectious phenotypes + an unresolved paralog-confounding problem' does not amount to COPD-relevant evidence, and the malaria/Takayasu citations should not be allowed to read as COPD-supportive in synthesis even though they are individually accurate."

Non-candidate gene flagged by red-team-skeptic: FCGR3B

"This is formally out of scope as a 'candidate' here, but I don't think it can be silently set aside. Open Targets' own cross-trait L2G evidence and disease-association score (genetic_association 0.433, highest at the locus) consistently favor FCGR3B over both listed candidates, and FCGR3B is neutrophil-expressed — directly relevant to the neutrophilic-inflammation axis of COPD (prior #3), which neither HSPA6 nor FCGR3A biology addresses. Combined with HSPA6 never being top L2G gene in any indexed study and FCGR3A's paralog-confounding problem, the aggregate picture is that the true causal gene at this locus may not be on the candidate list at all. I'd flag this explicitly to synthesis-lead as a candidate-gene-selection gap warranting a follow-up look at FCGR3B (and joint FCGR2A/FCGR3A/FCGR3B CNV-aware fine-mapping literature) before finalizing this locus, rather than forcing a choice between HSPA6 and FCGR3A alone."
