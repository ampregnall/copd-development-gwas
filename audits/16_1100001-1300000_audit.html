CONTESTED gene: CACNA1H

Skeptic's full reasoning (verbatim from `evidence_tables/_status/16_1100001-1300000/critique.md`):

The fine-mapping case (PIP 0.767 lead + 0.98 cumulative credible-set mass, two missense variants) is the strongest statistical signal at this locus, but the researcher's own search found **zero** literature connecting CACNA1H to COPD, lung function, or airway/immune biology — this is a real and important negative, not just an absence of positive evidence. Everything else known about CACNA1H is neuro-endocrine (T-type calcium channel; familial hyperaldosteronism gain-of-function, epilepsy pharmacogenomics), and the one credible-set missense variant with a phenotype association (rs61734410, ethosuximide response in absence epilepsy, Glauser 2017) **failed to replicate** in Myers 2021 — a direct non-replication of the only variant-specific phenotypic claim in the credible set. The OT context sharpens the concern further: no genetic_association-datatype COPD score exists for CACNA1H (only clinical/literature text-mining, which likely reflects nearest-gene assumption in curated databases rather than independent genetic evidence), and the actual molecular-QTL colocalizations at this variant are with eosinophil/leukocyte counts and, notably, **smoking cessation** (h4 0.92–0.95) — not lung function or COPD. Given CACNA1H is a calcium channel with plausible neurobiological roles in reward/addiction pathways, this coloc pattern is a live alternative explanation (smoking-behavior mediation, prior #1) that the researcher flagged but could not corroborate with peer-reviewed literature. That gap cuts both ways: it's not confirmed, but it's also not refuted, and "no literature found" for smoking-CACNA1H is a weaker disconfirmation than an actual negative study. Overclaiming check: the researcher did not overclaim — they explicitly reported the null PubMed search and the non-replication. The risk is downstream, if synthesis-lead over-weights CACNA1H purely on PIP without registering that literally none of the human phenotype literature for this gene is pulmonary, and the one credible-set-variant phenotype claim didn't replicate. **Verdict: CONTESTED.** Not because a paper argues "CACNA1H is not the COPD gene here," but because the combination of (a) exclusively neuro-endocrine established biology, (b) failed replication of the only variant-level phenotype claim, (c) OT coloc pointing to smoking-cessation/blood-cell-count traits rather than lung function, and (d) zero genetic_association-based COPD/lung evidence anywhere, together constitute credible grounds to contest treating high fine-mapping PIP as equivalent to pulmonary-disease relevance at this locus.

WEAKENED gene: TPSG1

Skeptic's full reasoning (verbatim):

Important correction to a possible overclaiming pattern, in the researcher's favor: Hansbro 2014's Prss31 knockout is genuinely the mouse ortholog of TPSG1 (TPSG1's protein product, transmembrane tryptase gamma, is also called PRSS31), so this is legitimate gene-specific evidence, not family-level bait-and-switch. However, it is a single mouse loss-of-function study with a **double-edged phenotype**: protective for smoke-induced macrophage infiltration/fibrosis but harmful (increased) for baseline airway hyperreactivity. A knockout that improves one COPD-relevant endpoint while worsening another cannot cleanly support a directional hypothesis ("TPSG1 loss is protective" or "risk-increasing") for human COPD — this needs to be stated plainly to synthesis-lead rather than folded into a single "most mechanistically relevant" framing. The surrounding tryptase-family literature (Beckett 2013 on mast cell protease-6, Siddhuraj 2022 on CPA3, Wang 2025 review, Liu 2026 on general tryptase+ mast cells) genuinely does **not** test TPSG1 — these are different genes/proteins (beta-tryptase, MCPT6, CPA3) in a related but distinct pathway. The researcher labeled this correctly as "family-level, not gene-specific," which is appropriate, but there is a real risk that a downstream reader treats five tryptase-pathway citations as convergent evidence for TPSG1 when only one (Hansbro) actually tests the gene. This should be flagged as a **volume-vs-specificity overclaiming risk** even though the researcher's own framing was careful. Structurally, TPSG1 has **no credible-set variant** at all (PoPS is an orthogonal, expression/similarity-based method, discordant here with fine-mapping, which puts 98% of PIP mass on CACNA1H). This PoPS-vs-fine-mapping conflict is a genuine, unresolved tension that should be surfaced to synthesis-lead rather than implicitly resolved by treating TPSG1 as a co-equal candidate. OT confirms TPSG1's pulmonary-disease and COPD associations are animal_model + literature only, with no genetic_association datatype support, and the gene is not LoF-constrained (oe=2.00, tolerant-looking), which is at least weakly consistent with being a less essential/less disease-driving gene, though not dispositive on its own. **Verdict: WEAKENED.** The single Hansbro mouse study is real, gene-specific, and worth reporting, but the inference that TPSG1 is a strong candidate overreaches: the phenotype is double-edged, there is zero human genetics support, the surrounding tryptase-family papers don't test this gene and shouldn't be counted as corroboration, and PoPS discordance with the fine-mapping (which overwhelmingly favors CACNA1H) is unresolved.
