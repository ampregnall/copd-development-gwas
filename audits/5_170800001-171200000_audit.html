Gene marked CONTESTED / WEAKENED by red-team-skeptic (full reasoning, verbatim from critique.md)

RANBP17 — WEAKENED

"The researcher's core claim — 'no plausible direct mechanistic link to airway/alveolar structural biology or immune/inflammatory COPD pathways' — is well supported by the verified citations, which uniformly describe nucleocytoplasmic transport, testis biology, neurodevelopment/aging, and DYT1 dystonia, with literally zero lung, airway, or COPD-relevant hits despite explicit targeted searches. That negative-evidence claim is appropriately hedged (the researcher says 'no PubMed literature found,' not 'RANBP17 is not involved in lung disease').

The smoking-mediation hypothesis, however, is exactly as unconfirmed as the researcher says it is, and I'd push the hedge even further than they did. The chain of reasoning is: (1) Open Targets shows RANBP17 as dominant L2G gene at rs6880972 across ~20+ smoking-trait credible sets; (2) Liu 2019 and Xu 2020 are the plausible source GWASs; (3) but the researcher 'could NOT confirm RANBP17 is named/ prioritized as the credible-set gene in either paper.' That's a real gap — Open Targets' own L2G pipeline, not a literature claim, is doing all the work here. L2G scores are model-derived, not literature-verified associations, and this locus sits in a gene-dense region (RANBP17/NPM1/KCNIP1/ TLX3/GABRP all within range) where an eQTL hitting multiple tissues (liver, brain caudate, monocyte, placenta, NK cells per the database context) is at least as consistent with RANBP17 being a broadly-expressed passenger gene near the causal regulatory element as with it being the true smoking-behavior effector gene. ... No overt overclaiming here — the hedging is adequate — but I'd want synthesis-lead to state explicitly that 'smoking-mediated via RANBP17' is an inference from L2G colocalization patterns, not from any smoking-GWAS paper naming the gene.

RANBP17: WEAKENED — not because the researcher overclaimed, but because the evidence structure itself (LoF-tolerant, no lung biology, smoking-signal attribution unconfirmed in primary literature, dense LD neighborhood with NPM1/KCNIP1/TLX3 also plausible at the same variant) means RANBP17's candidacy for *any* COPD mechanism — smoking-mediated or direct — rests on database-level colocalization inference rather than confirmed literature or spirometry concordance. This should be reported to synthesis-lead as a weak, indirect case, best framed as 'signal is more consistent with smoking/adiposity pleiotropy than lung biology' rather than 'RANBP17 causes smoking behavior.'"

FGF18 — CONTESTED

"1. Physical location outside the locus. FGF18's gene body (chr5:171,419,637-171,457,626) is ~220 kb outside the strict credible-set window (170,800,001-171,200,000)... FGF18 is a candidate purely by MAGMA/PoPS window-based gene-scoring, not because any credible-set variant sits in or near it. ... MAGMA/PoPS gene-level scores are a different (and much fuzzier) form of evidence than L2G/credible-set proximity.

  1. Unresolved-provenance COPD L2G score. The 0.224 COPD L2G score ... comes from Open Targets rows whose study/variant provenance could not be resolved. This means we cannot even confirm this score derives from the same locus/lead variant under review. ... without provenance, this score cannot be used as evidence linking *this locus's* credible set to FGF18-mediated COPD risk.
  1. Developmental-to-adult extrapolation gap. This is the crux weakness. Every single mechanistic paper ... is fetal, neonatal, or postnatal-developmental ... or, in the IPF case, a restrictive fibrotic disease, not obstructive COPD. None of this is adult emphysema/COPD biology. ... the one candidate paper that might have bridged this gap (Duong 2022, alveologenesis atlas) was CLAIM_MISMATCH-rejected by the citation-verifier — the abstract does not name FGF18 as the lineage marker. That rejection is itself informative: it's a second data point ... suggesting the FGF18-COPD link is being reconstructed by inference/analogy rather than found directly in the literature.
  1. No spirometry/FEV1 concordance (prior #2) is reported for FGF18 at all — this is a real absence, not just an omission, and per the priors this should count as a caveat against prioritization, not a neutral gap.

Taken together, I do think the researcher is mildly overclaiming when they write 'FGF18 has much stronger mechanistic/structural biology plausibility than RANBP17' as if this settles the ranking. That sentence is true only for *lung developmental biology in general*, not for *this GWAS locus specifically* ... The developmental mechanism is real and worth reporting, but the case that it explains *this* GWAS signal is considerably weaker than the 'much stronger... than RANBP17' framing implies — it's a plausible-biology, weak-genetics case, versus RANBP17's implausible-biology, moderate-genetics (via L2G/eQTL) case. ...

FGF18: CONTESTED — not because I found an external paper actively arguing against FGF18, but because the researcher's own assembled evidence contains an internal contradiction between the gene's genuine (but developmental/neonatal, not adult, and partly fibrosis- not COPD-context) mechanistic plausibility and the genetic evidence tying it to *this* locus, which is weak on multiple independent grounds (out-of-window physical position, minor L2G status at the lead variant, unresolved-provenance COPD score, and a rejected bridging citation). I'd ask the researcher/synthesis-lead to explicitly separate 'FGF18 is a strong developmental-lung-biology gene in general' from 'FGF18 explains the rs6880972 credible-set signal,' because the current write-up conflates the two more than the underlying evidence supports."

Alternative-gene / linkage-artifact consideration (verbatim)

"Given: (1) a 400kb region with 278 near-equally-weak credible-set variants (max PIP 0.065) all noncoding, (2) at least five plausible genes in cis (RANBP17, NPM1, KCNIP1, TLX3, GABRP) plus FGF18 pulled in from outside the window, and (3) rs6880972 itself showing strong multi-trait pleiotropy (smoking, adiposity, CRP, HDL, educational attainment, ADHD, restless legs) rather than a clean COPD/ spirometry signal — this locus has the classic signature of a broad, low-confidence, pleiotropic regulatory hotspot rather than a single well-resolved causal gene. NPM1 (COPD L2G 0.056, per the database context) is weak but nonzero and deserves at least a sentence of consideration rather than exclusion by fiat; KCNIP1 and TLX3 show zero COPD evidence and can reasonably be deprioritized. ... I'd flag to synthesis-lead that this locus may warrant a 'low confidence / pleiotropic hub, gene assignment uncertain' designation rather than crowning either candidate."
