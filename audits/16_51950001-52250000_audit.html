Genes marked CONTESTED

C16orf97 (=LINC02911) — CONTESTED

The single PubMed hit under this symbol (Dong et al. 2016, olfaction in African-American older adults, PMID 27878761) is about a different phenotype in a different population and does not itself validate the C16orf97=LINC02911 identity claim used to route MAGMA/PoPS prioritization here. More importantly, the locus background note that LINC02911's coordinates (52,005,486-52,078,516) almost entirely overlap the span the pipeline separately assigned to "LINC00919" is a serious structural problem that the researcher's literature search did not surface or resolve — if these are the same regulatory region under two catalog names (NCBI/RefSeq vs Ensembl/GENCODE), then treating "C16orf97/LINC02911" and "LINC00919" as two distinct pieces of corroborating credible-set evidence double-counts a single signal, and the "MAGMA/PoPS-prioritized gene" claim may just be re-describing the LINC00919-annotated PIP mass under a different name. This needs direct coordinate/annotation reconciliation (not literature search) before any weight is placed on "C16orf97 is prioritized by MAGMA/PoPS" as if it were independent evidence layered on top of the LINC00919 credible-set mass. I'm marking this CONTESTED not because I found a paper arguing against it, but because the pipeline's own background section already surfaces a naming-collision problem the researcher's writeup doesn't address or caveat.

**Orchestrator's direct follow-up (Ensembl REST coordinate lookup, structured database, not literature):** confirmed the coordinate overlap is real and essentially total. Ensembl's "LINC00919" gene body (ENSG00000260268) spans chr16:52,005,425-52,085,177 (GRCh38); NCBI RefSeq's LINC02911/C16orf97 spans chr16:52,005,486-52,078,516 -- the entire LINC02911/C16orf97 span (~73 kb) sits inside the Ensembl LINC00919 gene body. This upgrades the naming-collision concern from "plausible, unresolved" to "confirmed by direct coordinate lookup": C16orf97/ LINC02911 and the credible-set gene "LINC00919" are very likely the same underlying genomic feature described under two different gene-model catalogs, not two independently converging lines of evidence.

**Red-team-skeptic follow-up (second pass, after independent literature re-search):**

The naming-collision issue from the prior pass is unresolved (no literature disambiguating LINC02911 vs. LINC00919 vs. Ensembl coordinates was found). This pass adds a concrete illustration of the LD/nearest-gene-bias concern: GWAS Catalog's own automated "mapped gene" annotation for rs17201148 is C16orf97, even though the SNP is physically intronic to LINC02180 — i.e., the very structured database the researcher relied on for gene-mapping evidence is itself inconsistent about which gene "owns" this variant. Literature search adds only one tangential nicotine-dependence pleiotropy hit (PMID 33144568, not independently verified, off-topic keyword overlap only), no direct support.

**Status: remains CONTESTED.** Not excluded from the main table (its one literature citation, PMID 27878761, was independently VERIFIED by citation-verifier as a real, accurately-described finding), but flagged prominently in the evidence table's "skeptic verdict" column and in the Prioritized Gene section given the unresolved double-counting risk this creates for the overall MAGMA/PoPS-vs-credible-set convergence narrative at this locus.
