Audit Report - Locus 17:45850001-46100000 (nearest gene MAPT)

This is the audit trail for evidence_tables/17_45850001-46100000.md: excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH — none found at this locus, see below) and every gene the red-team-skeptic marked CONTESTED, with reasoning preserved verbatim from the checkpoint files. This locus required an unusually large pipeline effort (956-variant credible set, 8 candidate genes, 2 red-team-skeptic passes, 2 citation-verifier passes) given its position in the 17q21.31 MAPT/KANSL1/CRHR1/STH/SPPL2C inversion (H1/H2) haplotype block — the largest credible set in the dataset.

Excluded citations (RETRACTED / NOT_FOUND / CLAIM_MISMATCH)

**None.** Both citation-verifier passes (38 citations in pass 1, 4 additional citations in pass 2 — 42 total) returned VERIFIED for every citation checked. No citation was excluded from the evidence table on verification grounds. Several citations carried LIMITED RELEVANCE / low-weight caveats (not exclusion) — these are retained in the main evidence table with their skeptic verdicts (e.g. HOLDS as "correctly discounted, low-relevance evidence") rather than excluded, since the researcher and skeptic both handled them appropriately (reporting weak/ irrelevant findings honestly rather than overclaiming). For completeness, these are:

CONTESTED gene determinations (verbatim reasoning from critique.md)

MAPT — CONTESTED

From Pass 1 (search-budget-exhausted internal-consistency critique): "Real evidence (Morrow 2018 lung-tissue eQTL/network-integration top hit) exists, but it's a single small study (n=117) without stated independent replication, and the researcher is transparent that disambiguation from MAPT's dominant neurobehavioral biology is unresolved. Two of the citations used as supporting context (Shrine 2019, Sakornsakolpat 2019) are explicitly flagged by the citation-verifier as not confirmed to name this locus at all — presenting them alongside Morrow 2018 as part of MAPT's evidentiary weight, even loosely, risks overclaiming... Alternative explanation: MAPT's OT genetic-association component is higher for smoking initiation (0.366) and risk-taking (0.431) than for COPD (0.226), and it is 'frequently top L2G for neuroimaging/sleep traits' — consistent with a smoking/behavior-mediated pathway rather than direct pulmonary biology... LD confound: MAPT hosting the lead variant and majority of the 956-variant credible set is exactly what near-complete LD across an inversion block predicts regardless of which gene is causal — this is not independent evidence of MAPT's causal role... WEAKENED — real mechanistic hint, but the inference of MAPT as the pulmonary-relevant gene overreaches given unconfirmed GWAS-naming citations, dominant competing neurobehavioral signal, and LD centrality being non-diagnostic."

KANSL1 — CONTESTED

From Pass 1: "Wain et al. 2015 (UK BiLEVE, n=50,008, smoking-stratified design) is the most COPD/FEV1-trait-specific finding at this locus... However, this is a 2015 paper that predates modern fine-mapping; 'novel locus' naming conventions from that era typically reflect nearest-gene assignment within an LD block, not proof that KANSL1 specifically (versus MAPT or SPPL2C, all in the same near-complete-LD inversion) is functionally causal — the paper cannot distinguish among locus genes. There is also an unresolved, quantitatively stark internal contradiction: Open Targets gives KANSL1 the lowest COPD association score of any gene in the window (0.027)... while giving it the highest smoking-initiation score (0.345) — the opposite pattern from what the 'smoking-independent pulmonary mechanism' narrative would predict... Calling this 'the single strongest primary-literature finding at this locus for a direct, smoking-independent pulmonary mechanism' is an overclaim given that a single 2015 study, however well-designed, is contradicted by more recent aggregate genetic-association scoring for the very same trait... WEAKENED — the Wain et al. finding is real and the best FEV1-trait-specific evidence at the locus, but the researcher's superlative framing overreaches; the LD-block- attribution problem is not solved by a pre-fine-mapping paper, and the OT score tension is a legitimate unresolved contradiction that pulls in the opposite direction, not just a footnote."

From Pass 2 (follow-up, Koolen-de Vries syndrome check): "KdVS shows KANSL1 haploinsufficiency does perturb airway structure/function (tracheomalacia, recurrent respiratory infections), lending some plausibility to a developmental role consistent with Wain 2015's CNV-dosage finding — but the phenotype (structural malacia in a severe, complete-loss-of-one-copy syndrome) is not COPD-like airflow obstruction, so it doesn't transfer cleanly to a common-variant, adult-onset airflow-obstruction mechanism."

SPPL2C — CONTESTED, bordering artifact

From Pass 1: "Most internally inconsistent gene at the locus: highest COPD-specific Open Targets genetic-association component (0.329) despite only 3 low-PIP variants and zero direct COPD/lung/smoking literature. The one relevant mechanistic citation (Mentrup & Schröder 2021) explicitly attributes the plausible immune mechanism (CD74/MHC-II cleavage) to the PARALOGS SPPL2A/B and calls SPPL2C 'the least functionally characterized' of the three — the source cited to support SPPL2C's immune plausibility actually undercuts it. This pattern (high aggregated statistical score, minimal direct variant/PIP support, no gene-specific mechanism, discordant functional literature) is classic for an eQTL-colocalization or LD-driven statistical artifact rather than true causal-gene evidence — highly plausible in a 956-variant, near-complete-LD credible set... WEAKENED, bordering CONTESTED — I'd treat the OT COPD score for SPPL2C as more likely an LD/statistical artifact than genuine gene-specific signal until a mechanistic study exists."

CRHR1 — newly elevated third candidate (from Pass 2)

Verbatim Pass 2 finding: "the paper's pulmonary-fibrosis cross-phenotype-overlap discussion states verbatim: 'three segments shared with pulmonary fibrosis identified in the previous study (FAM13A, DSP, and the 17q21 locus – here nearest CRHR1).' This is a significant, previously-unrecorded finding: the dedicated 82-locus COPD case-control GWAS's own nearest-gene annotation for this region, in its pulmonary-fibrosis-overlap analysis, is CRHR1 — a THIRD candidate gene distinct from both MAPT and KANSL1. This directly weakens confidence in any single-gene call at this locus: three major papers touching this region give three different 'implicated gene' answers (Wain 2015 -> KANSL1, Shrine 2019 -> MAPT, Sakornsakolpat 2019 -> CRHR1), consistent with LD-block/nearest-gene-annotation instability rather than convergent evidence."
