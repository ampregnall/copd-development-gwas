Genes marked CONTESTED by red-team-skeptic (reasoning preserved verbatim)

MIR137HG — CONTESTED

Two red-team-skeptic passes were run on this locus (the first pass's web-search budget was exhausted before any independent contradiction search could execute; a second, follow-up pass attempted the specific unresolved searches and also hit exhausted WebSearch budget, substituting a weaker Europe PMC full-text proxy search). Both passes concurred on CONTESTED for MIR137HG. Full verbatim reasoning from both passes:

---

**PASS 1 reasoning (verbatim):**

"1. **Overclaiming.** No overclaiming from the researcher here — if anything the researcher was appropriately conservative, explicitly labeling the absence of COPD/FEV1 genetics literature and declining to treat the macrophage-polarization triad as mechanistic support. That said, the underlying dossier is worth restating plainly: there is **no COPD or FEV1 genetic evidence for MIR137HG at all** — not weak evidence, zero. The only statgen hit (Ripke 2011, schizophrenia) is for a different disease entirely, at the same chromosomal position by coincidence of LD/genomic proximity, not because MIR137HG has any established respiratory role.

  1. **Contradictory immune literature.** The three macrophage-polarization papers (Cao 2025, Liu 2023, Xu 2022) actively work against, not for, MIR137HG as a coherent immune-mechanism candidate at this locus: they disagree with each other on direction of effect (pro-M2/anti-inflammatory in acute lung injury vs. pro-M1/pro-inflammatory in glioma), are drawn from three unrelated disease contexts (none of them COPD or even chronic airway disease), and one (Cao et al.) is the only one with any lung-relevance at all (acute lung injury, an acute/reversible model, mechanistically distinct from COPD's chronic remodeling/obstruction). This is a genuinely 'mixed/contested' evidence pattern, correctly characterized as such by the researcher, and should not be used to argue for an immune mechanism.
  1. **LD/positional bias.** MIR137HG is the textbook case the priors ask reviewers to watch for: it physically overlaps the majority (~28/47) of credible-set variants by sheer virtue of being large and sitting where the LD block is, but every single one of those variants is low-PIP, non-coding-transcript/intronic, and MODIFIER impact, and critically, no computational prioritization method (FLAMES/MAGMA/PoPS/ABC) picked MIR137HG over DPYD despite that variant density. That's a meaningful negative signal from the tools that do incorporate regulatory/expression evidence, not just an absence-of-literature problem — it suggests the tools looked at this and didn't find MIR137HG-specific support even though it's the more variant-dense gene in the region.
  1. **Should MIR137HG be CONTESTED?** I'd actually push back slightly on defaulting to CONTESTED in the strict sense the framework intends (i.e., 'credible contradicting evidence found') — I did not find a paper that argues FOR MIR137HG and against which a contradicting paper exists; rather I found an absence of any positive case to begin with, plus internally-contradictory indirect evidence. This is better characterized as WEAKENED-to-unsupported than CONTESTED in the adversarial sense, but given the instructions treat contested/mixed indirect evidence as grounds for CONTESTED, and given the three macrophage papers directly conflict with each other on mechanism/direction, I'll mark it CONTESTED on the basis of that internal contradiction, while noting clearly that this is contradiction within weak indirect evidence, not a head-to-head rebuttal of a specific MIR137HG-COPD claim (because no such specific claim exists to rebut).

**Verdict: CONTESTED** (specifically: contested/non-specific indirect immune evidence, zero direct genetic evidence, unanimous non-prioritization by computational fine-mapping tools despite physical variant overlap). This is a weaker candidate than DPYD overall, not a stronger one — being 'not selected by any prioritization method' is itself a strong signal against it.

**What would resolve it:** any COPD or FEV1 GWAS colocalization or eQTL evidence showing MIR137HG expression is modulated by credible-set variants in lung or lung-immune cell types; a within-locus fine-mapping analysis (e.g., functional fine-mapping using lung eQTL/ATAC-seq) that can adjudicate whether the low-PIP MIR137HG-overlapping variants have independent regulatory activity from the DPYD-annotated variants, versus simply being in LD with the DPYD signal (most likely scenario given PIP distribution — the lead variant and all high-PIP variants are DPYD-annotated)."

---

**PASS 2 follow-up reasoning (verbatim):**

"**MIR137HG: CONTESTED (verdict unchanged).** My proxy searches found zero evidence connecting MIR137HG to lung function, COPD, or smoking-behavior GWAS traits — its only well-replicated GWAS signal remains schizophrenia (consistent with the original researcher's framing). This is consistent with, and does not overturn, the prior CONTESTED call; if anything it reinforces the concern that if MIR137HG (rather than DPYD) is the true effector gene at this locus, the mechanism is far more likely psychiatric/behavioral (potentially smoking-initiation-adjacent via shared psychiatric-genetic architecture) than a direct pulmonary one — but I found no direct GSCAN/Saunders hit to confirm even that pathway, so this remains speculative, not newly substantiated."
