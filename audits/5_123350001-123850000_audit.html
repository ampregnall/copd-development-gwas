Excluded citation: CLAIM_MISMATCH

**PMID 37547106 — Dong Y et al., 2023, Frontiers in Pediatrics, DOI: 10.3389/fped.2023.1102639**

- Researcher's original claim (as submitted to citation-verifier): "Clinical/genetic case series of 36 children with Joubert syndrome, noting that JBTS patients commonly show 'breathing abnormalities' as part of the clinical phenotype (oculomotor apraxia plus episodic hyperpnea/apnea) and multi-organ involvement. This particular series's genetic findings were in other JBTS genes, not CEP120 specifically — cited only as general JBTS-phenotype context, not as CEP120-specific genetic evidence." - Citation-verifier verdict: **CLAIM_MISMATCH**. Verbatim: "Paper exists with correct DOI (10.3389/fped.2023.1102639) and is a JBTS case series documenting breathing abnormalities. However, the claim states 'genetic findings were in other JBTS genes, not CEP120 specifically.' This is inaccurate: the abstract explicitly reports identification of a CEP120 variant (c.214 C>T, p.Arg72Cys) as one of their novel findings. This is a gene-specific genetic finding in CEP120." - Red-team-skeptic's assessment of this mismatch: "This is not a case of the researcher overclaiming; if anything they under-claimed and mischaracterized the paper in the *opposite* direction (making CEP120 look less implicated than it is in that paper). However, this paper still only adds another rare-variant JBTS case, not COPD/FEV1/ common-variant evidence — so correcting the claim doesn't change the overall verdict on the architecture-gap problem. It should be re-submitted to the citation-verifier with a corrected claim ('Dong et al. report a CEP120 p.Arg72Cys variant in a JBTS proband with breathing abnormalities, part of a broader ciliopathy case series') before being used at all, and even then it remains rare-disease evidence, not GWAS-bridging evidence." - Disposition: excluded from the main evidence table per pipeline rule (only VERIFIED citations are used). If a future pipeline run wants to use the corrected claim above, it must be re-submitted to citation-verifier as a fresh, accurately-worded claim before inclusion — it is not carried forward here as verified.

No RETRACTED or NOT_FOUND citations were identified at this locus. All other 12 citations returned VERIFIED (see `evidence_tables/_status/5_123350001-123850000/verified_citations.md` for full per-citation verifier output).

Gene-level skeptic verdicts: WEAKENED / CONTESTED-adjacent

CSNK1G3 — WEAKENED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic: "The researcher did the right thing here: ran five targeted dedicated searches and got zero hits on every axis (COPD, lung function, epithelial development, immune/inflammatory, smoking). All four verified citations are genuinely off-target — kidney-stone MR, a Han-Chinese positive-selection scan, bovine milk-protein GWAS, and a cancer-cell-line kinome screen. None of these establish a lung mechanism, and none were stretched by the researcher into a lung claim (no overclaiming detected). The Open Targets scores independently corroborate this: COPD L2G 0.096, FEV1 L2G 0.081/0.079 — all weak... intronic location is not evidence of function on the harboring gene; introns of one gene very commonly contain regulatory elements (enhancers, etc.) acting on a neighboring gene (classic FTO/IRX3 pattern), so this doesn't resolve the question either way. The absence of any missense/splice variant in the credible set removes the strongest form of direct-coding evidence for CSNK1G3... Verdict: WEAKENED. The verified literature is real but entirely irrelevant to lung biology; the statistical (Open Targets) evidence is also weak for this gene specifically. I found no contradicting paper naming a different mechanism for CSNK1G3 (nor could I search independently), so this isn't CONTESTED in the sense of an active counter-claim — it's a case of a nearest/intronic gene with no supporting mechanism despite genuine effort to find one."

CEP120 — WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED

Verbatim from red-team-skeptic (key excerpts; full text also in `evidence_tables/_status/5_123350001-123850000/critique.md`):

"**Architecture mismatch.** Every verified functional citation for CEP120 (Shaheen 2014, Joseph 2018, Roosing 2016, Tsai 2019) is about **rare, biallelic, loss-of-function or hypomorphic missense mutations** causing a distinct monogenic ciliopathy spectrum (JATD, Joubert syndrome, Meckel syndrome). That is a fundamentally different genetic architecture from the credible-set signal actually being evaluated here: a common noncoding variant (rs35390568, PIP 0.092, downstream_gene_variant, MAF presumably common in a population GWAS). None of the four Mendelian-disease papers touch common variation, heterozygous dosage effects, expression modulation, or any regulatory mechanism at this specific locus. The literature does not bridge that gap — it is an analogy the researcher is implicitly relying on ('ciliogenesis relevant to airway mucociliary biology'), not a demonstrated mechanism.

**Phenotype mismatch, not just architecture mismatch.** JATD is a **restrictive** thoracic-cage disorder (small thorax constrains lung growth) — this is mechanistically distinct from COPD, which is airflow obstruction from airway/parenchymal disease. Citing JATD as 'supporting a pulmonary mechanism' without noting that the established human phenotype is restrictive rather than obstructive physiology is a meaningful gap in the researcher's narrative...

**Point 3 — alternative explanation for the L2G scores.** L2G-type models are trained on features like variant-to-gene distance, VEP consequence, chromatin interaction, and eQTL overlap — not literature-curated mechanism. A high L2G score for CEP120 could easily reflect CEP120 simply being the closest/only annotated protein-coding gene with a usable TSS-distance or chromatin-loop feature in that specific sub-region... The wide range of the 14 FEV1 L2G rows (0.052-0.840, with 4/14 rows below 0.73) is itself worth noting: this is not full consensus across whatever underlying studies/tissues feed that aggregation... That heterogeneity is evidence the signal is not unambiguous.

**Point 5 — unexplored alternative: KRT18P16 / unannotated regulatory element.** The researcher notes the CEP120-proximal credible-set variants sit near a pseudogene. Pseudogenes can host regulatory elements (enhancers, ceRNA function) independent of the nearby protein-coding gene's identity. Neither the researcher nor I could evaluate whether the region regulates KRT18P16, a currently unannotated element, or acts at a distance on CSNK1G3 via a chromatin loop. This alternative is not ruled out and should be named explicitly as an open possibility, not assumed away by the FLAMES/ABC prioritization (both of which have known false-positive-to-nearest-annotated-gene tendencies in gene-dense or pseudogene-adjacent regions).

**Verdict: WEAKENED, bordering on CONTESTED.** I am not marking it fully CONTESTED because I did not find (nor was I able to search for, given exhausted budget) a specific paper arguing FOR a different gene at this locus or presenting a null COPD/CEP120 finding — the 'contest' here is internal to the evidence: a real statistical prioritization signal (Open Targets L2G, FLAMES, ABC) sits alongside a genuinely disconnected literature base (rare monogenic ciliopathy, restrictive not obstructive, no common-variant/regulatory bridge, one CLAIM_MISMATCH). The inference 'CEP120 causal via ciliopathy/mucociliary mechanism' as currently written overreaches: it should be reframed as 'CEP120 is the better-supported gene by aggregated statistical fine-mapping (L2G/FLAMES/ABC), with plausible but *unverified* mechanistic rationale from a distinct rare-disease phenotype; no literature currently connects the common regulatory variant at this locus to CEP120 expression, airway ciliary function, or COPD/FEV1 directly.'"
